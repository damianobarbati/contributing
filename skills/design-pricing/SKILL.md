# design-pricing

## Goal

Align pricing with customer value while keeping packaging clear and service economics sustainable.

## Scope

Cover new products, features, and existing plans. Use supplied business data; distinguish verified market facts from hypotheses.

## Workflow

1. Identify a value metric that scales with perceived customer benefit, such as active users, transactions, or processed volume. Check incentive alignment.
2. Compare direct competitors as category anchors. Consider switching costs and differentiated value without treating competitors' prices as absolute limits.
3. Prefer at most three distinct plans. Define inclusions, usage allowances, gating, overages, and a natural upgrade path.
4. Estimate service cost per customer or value unit. Check gross margins at typical and heavy usage, including infrastructure and support.
5. Evaluate migration effects for existing customers and identify assumptions to test before rollout.

## Rules

- Avoid metrics that punish useful adoption or reward behavior contrary to customer value.
- Avoid structural permanent discounts; prefer bounded trials or temporary incentives when appropriate.
- Remove confusing plan options. Explain the tradeoff when exceptions are required.
- Verify current competitor prices from authoritative sources when using them; do not invent market evidence.
- Do not publish pricing or change billing configuration without authorization.

## Verification

- Check that each plan serves a distinguishable customer need and has clear upgrade criteria.
- Check unit consistency, cost assumptions, heavy-usage scenarios, and margin calculations.
- Flag missing willingness-to-pay evidence, uncertain cost inputs, and untested conversion assumptions.

## Output

Return value metric, proposed plan comparison, price assumptions or ranges, cost and margin scenarios, migration implications, and validation steps. Separate measured data from recommendations.
